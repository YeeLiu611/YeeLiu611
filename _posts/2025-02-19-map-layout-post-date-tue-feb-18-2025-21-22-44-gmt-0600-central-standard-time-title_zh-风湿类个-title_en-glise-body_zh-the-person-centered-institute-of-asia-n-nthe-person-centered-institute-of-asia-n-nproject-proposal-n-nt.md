  THE PERSON-CENTERED INSTITUTE OF ASIA


  THE PERSON-CENTERED INSTITUTE OF ASIA


  Project Proposal


  This proposal outlines the background, mission, organizational structure, core projects, and future plans for The Person-Centered Institute of Asia (hereafter “the Institute”). It focuses on the development and needs of the Person-Centered Approach (PCA) in counseling, education, and research across China and other parts of Asia.


  1. Background and Current Situation

  2. PCA Development in China and Asia


  Lack of Formal Training Programs: In China, there are virtually no true Person-Centered Approach (PCA) training programs. Vietnam also lacks these resources. Japan and Korea have somewhat more established foundations, but the Institute will not immediately focus on these regions in the short term.


  Mislabeling in Existing “Person-Centered” Initiatives: Certain organizations, including major psychology associations in China, have promoted “Person-Centered” training projects. However, many instructors come from psychodynamic or CBT backgrounds and lack a genuine understanding of PCA, creating frustration among students who truly value the PCA philosophy.


  Practice and Certification Challenges:


  Historically, the Chinese government issued a psychology license with a very low entry threshold, leading to around 1.5 million license holders, many without adequate competency to practice counseling. The license was discontinued in 2017, leaving the industry essentially unregulated.


  Despite the lack of government oversight, counselors often still need professional association recognition or credentials to practice or seek employment. International certification can be more aligned with PCA but requires further support and promotion within China.


  Insufficient Research:


  Published PCA research from Asia, especially Chinese-speaking regions, is scarce. Most research still comes from Europe, North America, and South America. There is an urgent need to support local researchers, build PCA research communities, and develop an Asia-focused body of literature.


  2. Rationale for Establishing the Institute


  Addressing Local Demand: Students who deeply appreciate PCA struggle to find authentic programs in China or neighboring countries.


  Enhancing Professional Standards: With the counseling field in China lacking regulation and clarity, a high-quality, authentically PCA-based training and community can help reduce misinterpretation.


  Building a Regional Network: While initially focused on China, the Institute ultimately aims to serve other parts of Asia, linking PCA counselors, educators, and practitioners across cultures.


  Long-Term Vision: Promote person-centered values in education reform, conflict mediation, and human rights issues within Asia.


  2. Organizational Positioning and Structure


  \-. Legal Registration and Nature The Institute intends to register in Hong Kong as a company and establish an institute branch, making it easier to balance nonprofit and for-profit operations. /. Management Approach The Institute favors a decentralized, community-driven model, with members organizing activities and volunteers providing technical support.


  1. International Connections


  The Institute seeks membership in PCE-World for professional backing and a global network to promote the growth of PCA.


  3. Corethe Projects and Implementation Plan


  Currently,


  Institute has four primary programmatic areas:


  1. Counselor Training Programs


  (1) Tiered Course Structure


  Short Workshops: Designed for newcomers to PCA or practicing counselors seeking a foundational experience. Introductory Course on Person-Centered apporach:


  Expands on the workshops, introducing the full picture and its contemporary situation Comprehensive Person-Centered Counseling Training:


  Adds ethics and legal topics for those aiming to practice in China.


  Offers a full, systematic curriculum including practicum and supervision. International Recognition and Certification:


  Pursue collaboration with recognized bodies Adopt a flexible grading model where learners can self-assess if needed for transcripts, while those who do not require grades can opt out.


  (2) Delivery Modes and Faculty Primarily Online:


  Conduct lectures and interactions via Zoom to avoid censorship and geographical barriers.


  Use a dedicated website and social media platforms (WeChat, Facebook) to distribute materials. Physical Venue (Future):


  Plan to establish an offline center to host workshops, training camps, and encounter groups. Person-Centered Teaching:


  Emphasize encounter groups, self-directed exploration, and genuine communication.


  Core faculty will be local PCA specialists for cultural and linguistic compatibility, with periodic guest lectures from international experts.


  2. Online Community Building


  (1) Platforms and Technology


  Zoom: Central tool for virtual events and courses.


  Official Website: Sharing project information, resources, and community access. Social Media:


  WeChat (for mainland China) Facebook (for broader Asia and global Chinese-speaking communities)


  (2) Community Operation and Activities Decentralized Management:


  Activities initiated by members; volunteers offer technical and logistical support. Encounter Groups:


  The core of the community, typically 2-3 gatherings per month.


  Participants engage in open dialogue, promoting authenticity, empathy, and mutual respect. Multilingual Development:


  Currently focused on Chinese, but potential future groups in Vietnamese, Korean, Japanese, etc. Ongoing Support System:


  A space where students and practitioners can continually share cases, resources, and emotional support.


  3. Research Support (Future Vision)


  (1) Research Areas and Objectives


  Apply PCA within Asian cultural settings.


  Explore PCAʼs role in political contexts and human rights in Asia.


  Develop research and theory from an Asian perspective, contributing to international discourse.


  (2) Planned Measures


  Establish a Fund or Foundation:


  Provide grants or targeted funding for PCA research projects. Create an Asian PCA Research Community:


  Foster cooperation among researchers, sharing data, case studies, and findings. Academic Conferences and Journals:


  Aim to launch or support an Asia-focused PCA journal.


  Organize PCA conferences in the region to encourage scholarly dialogue.


  4. Person-Centered Education (Future Vision)


  (1) Why Yunnan Dali


  Dali hosts one of the highest concentrations of innovative educational institutions in China.


  It is home to a branch of an international “humanistic” academy, offering potential collaboration. (2) Project Concept


  Develop a PCA-based approach for K-12 education (kindergarten through high school). Emphasize self-directed learning, the absence of traditional grading, and respect for individual growth.


  Serve as a pilot for broader educational reform, potentially scalable to other regions.

  \-. Membership in PCE-World Access international PCA networks, mentorship, and resources.


  Enhance professional credibility through global connections. /. Social Media and Word-of-Mouth Maintain a WeChat public account, Facebook page, and possibly Weibo to share PCA knowledge.


  Collaborate with local mental health forums and media outlets to increase visibility. 0. Public Events and Introductory Workshops Host free or low-cost events to allow potential students and the public to experience PCA. 1. Collaborations and Alliances Connect with counseling associations, educational institutions, and research centers to exchange resources and expertise.


  5. Phased Goals and Implementation Timeline

  6. Short-Term (Within 1 Year)


  Launch Counselor Training: Finalize course design and begin enrolling participants. Online Community: Establish the website and key social media channels; hold regular encounter groups (2-3 per month).


  Initial International Links: Apply for membership in PCE-World; seek professional guidance and recognition.


  2. Mid-Term (3-5 Years)


  Asian PCA Network:


  Form a cross-regional platform connecting PCA counselors and practitioners. Standardize and scale up encounter group activities, supported by a well-organized volunteer team. Research Support:


  Offer grants or small-scale funding to researchers focusing on PCA in Asia.


  Build partnerships with universities and research institutions to nurture local researchers. International Collaboration:


  Facilitate stronger ties between PCA communities in Asia and the rest of the world.


  3. Long-Term (5+ Years)


  Education and Conflict Mediation:


  Integrate PCA principles into educational reforms and conflict resolution settings, contributing to human rights advocacy. Foundation and Academic Ecosystem:


  Form a formal foundation, publish and disseminate PCA research in Asia.


  Host regular PCA conferences in Asia, establishing the region as an essential contributor to PCA scholarship. Broader Global Impact:


  Enable the Person-Centered Approach to flourish across Asia, influencing mental health, education, and social harmony worldwide.


  6. Resource Requirements and Risk Assessment

  7. Resource Needs Funding: Registration, platform development, faculty fees, and research grants. Faculty and Experts: Engage both local and international PCA specialists.


  Technology and Operations: Maintain online communities, websites, and media presence.


  Venue: Acquire or rent physical space for on-site workshops and encounter sessions. 4. Potential Risks Policy and Censorship: China s regulatory environment can be unpredictable; hence registering in Hong Kong and maintaining flexibility.


  Market Recognition: PCA remains relatively unknown in China; consistent education and promotion are necessary. Financial Instability: Early-stage funding may be limited Limited PCA Professionals: Short supply of authentic PCA-trained practitioners; time is needed to train local experts or invite external support.


  7. Conclusion


  The Person-Centered Institute of Asia is committed to authentic PCA philosophy across China and Asia by providing systematic training, a supportive community, and future-focused research and education initiatives. Starting with counselor training and online communitybuilding, we aim to expand into research support and educational innovation. By collaborating with international organizations (e.g., PCE-World) and leveraging partnerships both locally and globally, we aspire to significantly impact mental health, education, and human rights in Asia over the next five to ten years.
