## Introduction

Last autumn Annzo and I went to Chicago to visit our person-centred teachers. One afternoon we sat down to talk with Margret Warner. We asked her, "Why is there such a high percentage of homosexuals in the Person-Centered community? She said, 'Because in the 1950s, Carl Rogers' counselling centre was the only place in North America that did not consider homosexuality to be a disease. As she said this, something struck my heart and I suddenly sank into an indescribable emptiness and a deep sense of acceptance. In that moment, I seemed to see those who had been excluded, alienated and labelled 'sick' by mainstream society more than half a century ago, finally having a space where they did not have to pretend and were not humiliated. It was not the first time I had been deeply moved in this way. A few years ago, at the ADPCA conference, Gay Leah Barfield, then in her nineties and director of Rogers' 'Peace Project', told us a story: 'Many, many years ago, a former Nazi officer and a concentration camp survivor came to one of our meetings. At first they had an implacable hatred for each other. But by the end of the week they loved each other deeply. There was silence in the room. What was the force that made hatred dissolve, that made wounds heal, that made two people who should have been enemies fall in love?

Over the years I have thought about what is at the heart of a person-centred approach. There seem to be too many different answers to this question, but what must remain constant in all of them is a deep respect and trust for people, an attitude that treats people as people. To treat people as people and life as life is not easy. Growing up in China today, I don't really understand what it feels like to be allowed to be yourself. A person does not have the right to define himself, but is defined by exam results, marriage and childbirth, behavioural norms, wealth and status. Despite decades of economic boom, the phenomenon of 'not treating people as people' has intensified; in the crowd that chases the wind, there are always those who are ruthlessly left behind.

I remember when I was in primary school, my English teacher said in public, because I got a bad exam result: 'It's a shame for your parents to have given birth to such a stupid child'. I often imagine that if I were a primary school teacher today and I met a child with poor marks, I would pat them on the back and say: 'You didn't do well in the exam and I know you're very sad. It's really not easy. I just want you to know that I will be here with you. You are important. When our society treats people as a means to an end rather than an end in themselves, people cannot be themselves and relationships and attitudes are filled with cruelty rather than gentleness and warmth.

We live in a country full of deep historical trauma. The English teacher who humiliated me was not born with malicious intent. It's just that our history and culture have not given her a gentle soil in which to become deeply herself. As I met client after client in the counselling room who could not become themselves because of the barren environment, I began to understand why Rogers shifted his interest from counselling to a larger field in the second half of his life. Because if we don't change the world we live in, we can only do passive remedial work. But if we can influence everyone around us to have a little more space to be themselves, then their attitudes towards others will change a little. When a community has the space for everyone to be themselves, the collective will naturally develop the resilience to accept differences and the warmth to value humanity.

This is the vision of the Person-Centered Institute of Asia. The person-centred approach has never been just a model or a technique for counselling. It is a way of being, a fundamental view of life and humanity. It recognises that every life has irreplaceable value, no matter what form it takes. We treat a person in good health in the same way as someone in distress; we see someone similar to us in terms of their background in life in the same way as someone completely unrelated to us. There is so much light and possibility in human nature and we want to bring it out gently. I often think back to Rogers' efforts in the 1960s on issues of race, conflict resolution and educational liberation. Today, we hope to recapture that spirit in the Asian region - not just in counselling rooms, but in broader social and cultural contexts. And everyone reading this article has the opportunity to get involved.

# Introduction to The Person-Centered Institute of Asia

The full name of The Person-Centered Institute of Asia (PCIA) is The Person-Centered Institute of Asia. Our legal entity is registered in Hong Kong. It is a person-centred organisation for the whole of Asia. The PCIA WeChat subscription account is currently hosted by Karl Rogers (Shenzhen) Consulting Services Co., Ltd.

## Goal 1: To connect person-centred communities in the Asian region and around the world.

**Projects:** Asia Society, Person-Centered Congress in Asia, Person-Centered Asia Journal, Roundtable Forum

We have already made contact with representatives in Japan and Vietnam. In the future we will continue to make contact with communities in other Asian countries and regions. We plan to hold an annual person-centred conference in Asia, offering workshops, affinity groups and forums in different languages, so that person-centred practitioners from different countries have the opportunity to exchange ideas at a conference based in Asia. We plan to hold one or more roundtable forums, inviting person-centred practitioners from different countries to exchange ideas on a pressing issue of the day. We plan to establish an Asia-based person-centred academic journal within 3 years.

We have also contacted the Person-Centered World Association (PCE-World). The World Association will set up a working group to assist us in establishing an Asian Association for Person-Centered Counselling and Therapy. The Asian Association will take on functions such as accreditation of training programmes, registration and accreditation of counsellors, and practice support.

How you can get involved: In addition to registering for conferences, forums and future contributions to the journal, we welcome you to join us as a volunteer. ## Goal 2: Support the ideas and creativity of individuals and organisations

Projects: Person-centred incubator project, joint projects, internal foundation

We will establish an internally managed foundation at the Person-Centered Institute of Asia (PCIA). PCIA will allocate a percentage of the profits from all its projects to this foundation and will also accept donations. The Foundation will be open and transparent and its financial reports will be published regularly.

The Foundation will provide funding for the person-centred incubator. Any organisation or individual with an idea that is in line with person-centred values (whether it is caring for abandoned children, counselling or feminist workshops, or a piece of research) can apply to the PCIA for support. The PCIA will provide support in the form of, but not limited to, personnel support, financial support, project supervision and international links, depending on the actual funds and resources available. The project does not have to be very ambitious. It may even be that an independent counsellor wants to provide free counselling to a particular disadvantaged group for a certain period of time, but needs additional funding to cover the loss of income.

Incubator projects can also be for-profit (for example, a counsellor wants to charge for a group or workshop). We will provide the same support to for-profit projects, while charging a proportion of the project's profits to support the day-to-day running of PCIA and to go into the Foundation to support other non-profit projects.

How you can get involved: You can apply for projects as an individual or organisation, donate to our foundation or volunteer in our incubator project.

## Goal 3: Support the learning and growth of person-centred counsellors

Projects: Asian Association of Person-Centered Consultants registration system and project certification system, 2-year systematic training in person-centred consulting, introductory course on the basics of person-centred consulting, person-centred group supervision and person-centred groups.

We will establish a consultant registration system and a project certification system at the Asia Person-Centered Association to provide a certain 'endorsement' and organisational guarantee for the practice and development of person-centred consultants. In addition, we will begin planning for the establishment of a two-year long term training that is fully person centred. More immediate projects include introductory courses on the basics of person-centred counselling, appreciative groups, group supervision, etc. A percentage of the profits from for-profit projects in this sector will go to our foundation to support our non-profit projects.

How you can get involved: by attending our courses and trainings, by becoming a member of the Person-Centered Association of Asia in the future, by getting to know the registration and certification system that will be launched in the future, and by communicating with us and giving us your feedback.

## Goal 4: Work outside the professional field of counselling, such as education, interpersonal relationships, conflict resolution, etc.

Projects: Listening Space, Education Projects, Mediator Projects and more to come

We will prepare listening spaces, i.e. spaces for non-professionals and counsellors alike, so that more people can experience the power of listening. We will initiate new educational projects and to some extent encourage the development of free learning through cooperation with schools or educational communities. We will initiate a mediator project to utilise the benefits and traditions of person-centred approaches to conflict resolution and to support various conflicts that arise in the community through the training of mediators.

How you can get involved: Take part in our projects, join us as a volunteer, offer new project ideas or give us feedback and suggestions.
